An Xnapper alternative that works in any browser

Xnapper is a native macOS screenshot app. DevSnapKit is a free web editor for screenshots you already have, so it also works on Windows, Linux and Chromebooks.

What Xnapper does well

Xnapper describes itself as “the beautiful screenshot app for Mac”. Because it captures the screen itself, it can do things a web page cannot: auto-balancing, automatic backgrounds, auto-redaction of sensitive information, OCR text copy, arrows and shapes, and resizing for social sizes. Its site says you pay once to remove the watermark, with plans listed from $29.99 that include a year of updates.

When DevSnapKit is the simpler choice

DevSnapKit does not capture your screen — you take the screenshot with your system tool (for example Win+Shift+S on Windows) and drop it into the studio. From there you add padding, a gradient, solid, pattern or image background, rounded corners, shadows and a device or window frame, then export at a preset such as 1200×630, 16:9 or 1:1.

There is nothing to install, free exports carry no watermark, and the optional Pro pass is a one-time $1. Images are processed in your browser and are not uploaded.

Questions

Does DevSnapKit work on Windows?

Yes. It runs in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, so Windows, macOS, Linux and ChromeOS all work.

Can DevSnapKit blur or redact parts of a screenshot?

Not automatically. Redact sensitive details before importing, or use a capture tool with redaction such as Xnapper.

Is there a watermark on free exports?

No. Free exports are clean. Only text you add yourself, such as an optional brand badge, appears on the image.
